**Subject:** A free cloud base forecast for the hills, and a thank you

Hi Paul and Helen,

I am a hillwalker and this is a hobby project rather than a business. I wanted
to explain what I have built, and ask whether it is of any interest. Please
feel free to say no.

The problem I kept having is this. Every weather app tells me it will be eight
degrees and windy on the summit. None of them tell me the thing I actually want
to know, which is whether I will be able to see anything when I get there.
Cloud has a bottom, and which side of that line a summit falls on decides
whether the day was worth the drive.

So I built hillweather.co.uk. It reads the atmosphere above each hill level by
level, works out the height of the cloud base, and compares it against the
summit's real surveyed height rather than the flattened height a 2 km weather
model sees. It does that twice a day for all 282 Munros, all 214 Wainwrights
and the Hewitts of Snowdonia. It is free, has no accounts and no advertising,
and I have no plans to change any of that.

Two things I want to be straight about.

First, I link to your route pages and never copy a word of them. 480 hills
carry a "Routes on Walkhighlands" link, each one checked so it cannot point at
a dead page. Your writing is your own work and scraping it would be both wrong
and lazy. Hill descriptions come from Wikipedia with attribution, and the hill
data from the Database of British and Irish Hills.

Second, I measured whether the forecast actually works rather than assuming it
did. Scored against observed cloud base, the first version was worse than
simply guessing the seasonal average, which I have written up openly. After
fixing it, it beats that baseline by about 26 per cent, and when it says a
summit will be clear it is right roughly nine times in ten. When it says a
summit is in cloud it is right about half the time, so it is far more useful in
one direction than the other, and the site says so. It has been checked against
measured cloud base at airfields, but not yet on summits themselves or through
a winter, and every page says that too.

I am not asking for anything in particular. If you thought it was useful to
your readers and wanted to mention it, that would be more than I expected. If
there is something that would make it genuinely more useful to Walkhighlands
readers, I would far rather build that than guess at it. And if the answer is
no, that is completely fine.

Either way, thank you for Walkhighlands. I have used it for years to plan
almost every hill day I have had.

## Notes before you send

**Timing.** This is stronger in October or November than in August. Nobody is
thinking hard about cloud base during a settled summer, and by autumn you will
have inversion mornings to point at, which is the part of the site that is
genuinely unlike anything else.

**Do not attach anything.** A link is enough. If they are interested they will
look.

**Expect a slow reply or none.** Two people run that site and the inbox will be
full. One follow-up after a month is fine; two is pestering.

**If they do reply, the useful question to ask** is which hills their readers
actually search for in winter. That would tell you what to build next better
than any amount of guessing.
